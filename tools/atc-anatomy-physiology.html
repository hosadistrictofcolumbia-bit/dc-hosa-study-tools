Read this before anything else. Only three topics, and two of them are 40 percent each — twenty questions apiece. Anatomy asks you to name and locate; physiology asks you to explain mechanism and regulation. They are the same twelve systems either way. Work system by system and you cover 80 percent of the test in one pass, with the 20 percent disease block hanging off the same order. HOSA supplies a basic handheld calculator; personal calculators are not allowed.

The official 26-27 test plan

Simulate the Test draws questions in exactly these proportions. Three topics is the flattest structure in the ATC set and the easiest to plan around.

Tiebreakers, dress, and how ATC entry works

In the event of a tie, an essay or tiebreaker question is judged. It is administered with the original test, inside the same 60 minutes — there is no separate window and no verbal warning at ILC, so budget for it before you start. ATC events award no bonus points for dress, unlike every other HOSA event, though you are still expected to look professional. And there are no qualification rules between state and ILC: you may take this test at your state conference, at ILC, at both, or at neither. ILC registration covers one ATC test; each additional test is $20, with no maximum.

Body Structures and Functions, 14th edition

National Geographic Learning / Cengage · ISBN 9780357457542 · the only official reference

Every item on this test is written from this book. It is a health-science A&P text rather than a college one, which means the level is reachable — but it also means the wording of a definition in this book is the wording the test uses. Read it, do not substitute a different A&P text.
