Read this before anything else. This is the most calculation-heavy test in the ATC set, and the calculator HOSA gives you does five operations only — add, subtract, multiply, divide, square root. No graphing calculator, no personal device. That constraint tells you what can be asked: mole conversions, molar mass, percent composition, empirical and molecular formulas, limiting reagent, percent yield, molarity and dilution, and gas laws. Practise those by hand, on paper, with a cheap calculator.

The official 26-27 test plan

Simulate the Test draws questions in exactly these proportions. Six topics sit at 4 percent — two questions each. Chemical reactions at 30 percent is worth more than all six of them combined.

Tiebreakers, dress, and how ATC entry works

In the event of a tie, an essay or tiebreaker question is judged. It is administered with the original test, inside the same 60 minutes — there is no separate window and no verbal warning at ILC, so budget for it before you start. ATC events award no bonus points for dress, unlike every other HOSA event, though you are still expected to look professional. And there are no qualification rules between state and ILC: you may take this test at your state conference, at ILC, at both, or at neither. ILC registration covers one ATC test; each additional test is $20, with no maximum.
