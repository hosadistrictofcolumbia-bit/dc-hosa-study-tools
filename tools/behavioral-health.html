Read this before anything else. Three quarters of this test is Cacioppo and Freberg's Discovering Psychology. The other quarter comes from SAMHSA and NAMI — and those questions are mostly statistics, which means they are memorizable in an afternoon. The official 26-27 test plan

Simulate the Test draws questions in exactly these proportions. Note that the first three topics are 25% each — three quarters of the test before you reach wellness, careers, or history.

How ties are broken

An essay tiebreaker is administered with the original test and written on the back of your answer sheet. You will not be given extra time for it and there is no separate announcement. Budget five minutes at the end of the hour. Ties are broken on that essay, so a blank back page can cost you a medal you already earned on the multiple choice.

Study Guide

Official References

These are the sources listed in the 2026-2027 guideline. Test items are written from them and nowhere else.

Cacioppo & Freberg — Discovering Psychology

Cengage Learning · latest edition · the primary source

The large majority of test items cite this book by page. Chapters on biological psychology, sensation and perception, consciousness, learning, memory, emotion, disorders, and therapies map almost one-to-one onto the first two topics of the test plan — which together are half your score.

BookPublisher page — confirm the current edition TopicMindTap companion — chapter quizzes and flashcards

SAMHSA — Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration

samhsa.gov · free · the source for the prevention and recovery topic

SAMHSA supplies the statistics, the treatment-system rules, and the prevention framework. These questions are highly memorizable — they are numbers and definitions, not reasoning. Learn the national survey figures and the medication rules and you bank most of a topic.

NAMI — National Alliance on Mental Illness

nami.org · free · advocacy, peer support, and condition basics

NAMI supplies the advocacy and support-structure questions — what a peer support group is, what NAMI Connection does, and the condition overviews written for the public rather than for clinicians.
