Read this before anything else. Reasoning is 24 percent of this test — the largest block — and it is not a body of facts you can memorize. It is troubleshooting logic: check power first, isolate the fault, verify with the right test instrument, document what you did. Medical devices and public safety add another 22 percent. Between them, nearly half the exam is about equipment behaving badly and what you do about it.

The official 26-27 test plan

Simulate the Test draws questions in exactly these proportions. Anatomy and physiology is only 10 percent here — enough to understand what a device is measuring and why a bad reading matters clinically, not a full A&P course.

The certification pathway is part of the point

In the event of a tie, the essay is judged. Worth knowing beyond the test: completing the two official courses lets HOSA members sit the AAMI Certified Associate of Biomedical Technology (CABT) exam at a reduced student rate, and the same coursework feeds registered apprenticeship programs through AAMI and American Healthcare Apprenticeship. This is one of the few competitive events with a direct credential attached.

The two official courses — one HOSA sign-in

AAMI BMET 101 and CBET MET 100 series · both reachable from a single login

Unlike most Health Science events, this one has no textbook. The reference list is two online courses, and HOSA set up one sign-in that reaches both. Completing them does more than prepare you for the test: it qualifies you to sit the AAMI Certified Associate of Biomedical Technology (CABT) exam at a reduced student rate, and counts as a pre-apprenticeship pathway into registered apprenticeship programs.
