Read this before anything else. Crime Scene and Death Investigation and Forensic Toxicology are 20 percent each — twenty questions between them. But Round Two is where teams separate: forty minutes with a case file, physical evidence, police reports and an autopsy, and you write a conclusion with supporting evidence on your own device. Rehearse that as a pair before the conference. Round One questions come only from Bertino and Bell — not from the medical references, which support Round Two.

The official 26-27 test plan

Simulate the Test draws from the eight official test-plan topics only. The Round Two section is in the study guide but carries no test weight — practise it separately from the timed simulation.

A note on content, and on who this event is for

The guideline is explicit: the material is graphic, advisors should get administrative and parental approval before a member competes, and the event is meant for advanced members who have already completed Medical Terminology and Anatomy & Physiology. Round Two rewards clinical, professional writing — the register of an autopsy report, not a true-crime podcast. Bring a personal device on battery power with a typing program and internet access, plus a second as backup. A school-managed device is not recommended.

Official References

These are the sources listed in the 2026-2027 guideline. Test items are written from them and nowhere else.

Bertino and Bertino — Forensic Science Fundamentals and Investigations

National Geographic Learning / Cengage · latest edition · a Round One source

Written for high school forensic science courses and closest to the level the test asks. Its chapter structure maps onto the test plan almost topic for topic.

Bell — Forensic Science

Taylor & Francis / CRC Press · latest edition · a Round One source

The more technical of the two, and stronger on toxicology, DNA methods and instrumentation. Where Bertino gives you the concept, Bell gives you the mechanism.
