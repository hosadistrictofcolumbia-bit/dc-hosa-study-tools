Start here. Word parts are 45 percent of this test — about sixteen of the thirty-five questions. Every other topic is worth 5 percent, which is one or two questions each. So learn the prefixes, roots, suffixes and combining forms first, second and third. A member who knows fifty word parts cold can do well on this test without memorizing a single body system.

The official 26-27 test plan

Simulate the Test pulls sixteen or so questions from word parts and one or two from each body system. That is the real shape of the exam.

How the test works

Round One is 35 questions in 60 minutes with a tiebreaker given in the same booklet, so budget time for it before you start. This is a Middle School Division event — you are competing against other middle schoolers, not against high school members.

Official References

These are the sources listed in the 2026-2027 guideline. Test items are written from them and nowhere else.

Stanhope and Turnbull — Introduction to Medical Terminology

Goodheart-Willcox · latest edition · the most approachable source

Written for beginners, which is what this event is for. Start here rather than with a college terminology text.

Ehrlich — Medical Terminology for Health Professions

Cengage · latest edition

The standard terminology text, also used by the high school event. More than you need for a 35-question test, but its word-part lists are excellent.
