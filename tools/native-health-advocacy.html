Read this before anything else. This is not a test about a minority population’s health outcomes. It is a test about sovereign nations and a federal trust obligation, and the health data follows from that. Competitors who learn the disparities but not the legal structure lose the Public Law 93-638, IHS Overview and referrals-ethics topics — which are 30 percent of the test between them. Start with the structure, then the numbers.

The official 26-27 test plan

Simulate the Test draws in exactly these proportions. Healthcare Barriers and Cancer Statistics are a third of the exam on their own, but the four structural topics — Public Law 93-638, IHS Overview, Minority Health and IHS Referrals Ethics — add up to 36 percent and they reward understanding over memorisation.

How ties are broken

Your final rank is the team’s averaged Round One test score plus the Round Two presentation score. If two teams tie, the higher averaged test score wins — so an uneven team is a real risk here in a way it is not in most events. Every member’s score counts toward the average that both qualifies you and breaks your tie.
