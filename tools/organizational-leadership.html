Read this before anything else. New for 26-27: the test is now 50 questions, up from 25. Voting Delegate Role and Parliamentary Procedure are 24 percent each — half the test — and parliamentary procedure is the half you can master completely, because Robert’s Rules is a fixed, published text. Learn the order of precedence and the debatable-amendable-vote table cold and you bank twelve questions before you study a single HOSA fact.

The official 26-27 test plan

Simulate the Test draws questions in exactly these proportions. History and Organizational Structure are 12 percent each and both are learnable in an evening from the HOSA website. Membership is 4 percent — two questions.

Why this event matters beyond the medal

HOSA created this event for two reasons it states outright: to help Chartered Associations pick Voting Delegates where no method is written down, and to make sure the members who represent their association at ILC actually understand the organization and can handle a business session. If your association has no written selection process in its Bylaws or Policies and Procedures, it may use this test as the method. Ask your State Advisor how DC does it.

Official References

Robert’s Rules of Order Newly Revised In Brief, 3rd edition

the named parliamentary authority · 24 percent of the test

Short, cheap, and completely masterable — roughly 200 small pages. HOSA’s own Bylaws name Robert’s Rules Newly Revised as the organization’s parliamentary authority, so this is not trivia; it is the rulebook of every business session you will sit in. This is the single highest-return purchase for this event.
