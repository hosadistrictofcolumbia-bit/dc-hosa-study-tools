Community Awareness

Your team picks one health or safety issue that matters where you live, runs a real awareness campaign in your community over the school year, and documents what you did and how many people it reached in a 12-page portfolio.

You submit
One combined PDF of the portfolio, uploaded by ONE member of the team to the HOSA Digital Upload System by the DC HOSA upload deadline. At the conference the team gives a presentation of no more than five minutes. Photo ID is required. Notecards, paper or electronic, are optional.
Rounds
Round One is the digital portfolio, pre-judged virtually before the conference. Round Two is a presentation to judges of no more than five minutes. Every team that uploads a portfolio by the upload deadline receives a Round Two appointment time. The portfolio score and the presentation score are added together to determine final results.
Dress
Proper business attire or official HOSA uniform. Bonus points will be awarded for proper dress. All team members must be properly dressed to receive the bonus points.

The DC HOSA calendar — read this before the guideline’s dates

Stage 1 · SLC — binding first

DC HOSA collects uploads and pre-judged material by the end of the first week of February. What you submit then must be final and complete — every required element, in the right format. It is judged exactly as submitted. The State Leadership Conference itself is in March, but the upload closes over a month before it. This is the deadline DC competitors miss.

Stage 2 · ILC — only if you qualify

Qualify at SLC and you may revise and improve the work. DC HOSA’s ILC upload deadline is generally around 1 May, deliberately ahead of the national date, so there is time to confirm every file landed in the right place and fix it if it did not.

For reference · the national deadline

The guideline states: "May 15 at midnight EST is the final deadline, and there will be NO EXCEPTIONS." That is HOSA's national ILC date for material submitted through the Digital Upload System.

Work to the DC dates, not this one. DC’s deadlines shift a little year to year — confirm the exact dates with your chapter advisor or the state advisor before you plan around them.

New for 2026-2027. Editorial changes have been made.
Read this before anything else. The campaign has to happen inside this year's window. The guideline is explicit: "The Chapter's campaign activities must be completed between July 1, 2026 – May 15, 2027." That means the food drive you ran last spring, the mental health week your chapter has done for three years running, and the partnership your advisor set up before you joined all count for nothing unless the activities you are documenting took place in that window and are dated inside it. Two consequences follow. First, date everything as you go — programs, photos, flyers, social posts, sign-in sheets. The guideline specifically says verification "should be included and dated," and a judge who cannot tell when something happened cannot credit it. Second, you cannot reconstruct reach after the fact. Count heads at the event, screenshot the post's view count that week, ask the newspaper for its circulation while the article is still fresh. The People Impacted section is worth real points and it is built entirely out of numbers you either wrote down at the time or did not.

    Round One scores the portfolio, pre-judged virtually before anyone meets your team. Round Two scores the presentation of no more than five minutes. "Scores from the pre-judged portfolio will be added to the presentation score to determine the final results." A tie is broken by the rating sheet section with the highest point value, then the next highest, in descending order. This extract does not include the rating sheet, so the exact point value attached to each criterion is in the official guideline PDF — get it and read it with your team.
