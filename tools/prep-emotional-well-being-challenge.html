Emotional Well-Being Challenge

Your team picks a mental health or substance use topic, builds a presentation for one specific audience, records it as a video, and builds a tool that measures whether the presentation actually changed what that audience knows and thinks.

You submit
Two things, uploaded by one team member: a public, shareable URL link to your recorded video presentation, and a PDF of the Summary of the Evaluation Plan.
Rounds
There is one round. Round One is the video presentation and the evaluation tool, pre-judged virtually. Pre-judged means judges score what you uploaded before the conference. You do not present live to judges. The extract describes no Round Two. It also says this event is only judged at the ILC level, not at the state association level.
Dress
The extract says nothing about attire, and it does not mention dress-code bonus points. That is a real silence, not something left out here. Your team members are visible in the recorded video, so dressing the way you would for any HOSA presentation is sensible — but treat that as good practice, not a rule, because the extract sets none. Check the full guideline PDF and the General Rules and Regulations before you assume either way.

The DC HOSA calendar — read this before the guideline’s dates

Stage 1 · SLC — binding first

DC HOSA collects uploads and pre-judged material by the end of the first week of February. What you submit then must be final and complete — every required element, in the right format. It is judged exactly as submitted. The State Leadership Conference itself is in March, but the upload closes over a month before it. This is the deadline DC competitors miss.

Stage 2 · ILC — only if you qualify

Qualify at SLC and you may revise and improve the work. DC HOSA’s ILC upload deadline is generally around 1 May, deliberately ahead of the national date, so there is time to confirm every file landed in the right place and fix it if it did not.

For reference · the national deadline

The guideline states: "The FINAL ILC digital upload deadline is May 15th," written as May 15, 2027 in the submission section, with no time of day given. It also warns: "We STRONGLY suggest not waiting until the last minute to upload online to avoid user challenges."

Work to the DC dates, not this one. DC’s deadlines shift a little year to year — confirm the exact dates with your chapter advisor or the state advisor before you plan around them.

Read this before anything else. Most teams build a good presentation and then run out of time on the evaluation. That order does not work here. The guideline asks for a written evaluation plan that shows your project succeeded, including pre- and post-data — information collected before your presentation and again after it. You cannot collect "before" data after you have already presented. So build your evaluation tool first, use it on your audience before you present, present, then use it again. The second thing that ends this event quietly: the video has to become a public, shareable link. If a judge cannot open it without signing in to an account, they cannot score it.
