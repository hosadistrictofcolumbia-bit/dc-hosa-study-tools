Extemporaneous Health Poster

You are handed a secret health topic at the conference, and you have three hours and your own art supplies to turn it into a poster that makes people understand something and want to act on it.

You submit
Nothing is uploaded. The guideline lists digital upload as NO for this event. You make the poster on site, at the conference, inside a three (3) hour window, and the finished poster is what gets judged. There is no file, no PDF, and no link. This extract does not say what size the poster has to be, what you draw on, or exactly how and where you hand it in when time is called. Those details live in the full guideline PDF and on the Competitor Equipment Check Sheet, so ask your advisor for both before the conference.
Rounds
Round One is poster creation, and it lasts three (3) hours. That is the only round this extract describes. There is no pre-judged upload before the conference, and no separate interview or presentation round is listed here. The word extemporaneous means you do not know the topic in advance. You get it when the round starts. Everything on your poster has to be made inside those three hours, in the room.
Dress
This extract says nothing about attire, and nothing about bonus points for dress. Do not assume there is a dress bonus, and do not assume there is not one. Ask your advisor and check the General Rules and Regulations, which apply to every HOSA event. There is also a practical side: you will be handling markers, paint, chalk or pastel for three hours, so whatever you wear is going to get worked in.

The DC HOSA calendar — read this before the guideline’s dates

Stage 1 · SLC — binding first

DC HOSA collects uploads and pre-judged material by the end of the first week of February. What you submit then must be final and complete — every required element, in the right format. It is judged exactly as submitted. The State Leadership Conference itself is in March, but the upload closes over a month before it. This is the deadline DC competitors miss.

Stage 2 · ILC — only if you qualify

Qualify at SLC and you may revise and improve the work. DC HOSA’s ILC upload deadline is generally around 1 May, deliberately ahead of the national date, so there is time to confirm every file landed in the right place and fix it if it did not.

For reference · the national deadline

The guideline lists digital upload as “NO,” so there is no national ILC upload deadline for this event. The only clock that exists is the three (3) hours in the room.

Work to the DC dates, not this one. DC’s deadlines shift a little year to year — confirm the exact dates with your chapter advisor or the state advisor before you plan around them.

New for 2026-2027. Blending stumps or cotton swabs have been added to the Competitor Equipment Check Sheet.
Read this before anything else. You cannot make this poster ahead of time. That is what extemporaneous means: the topic is secret until the round begins, and the entire poster is made in the room in three hours. So the one thing you can fully control before the conference is what is in your hands when the clock starts. HOSA publishes a Competitor Equipment Check Sheet for this event. That is the official list of what you bring and what you are allowed to use. The only change for 2026-2027 is on that list: blending stumps or cotton swabs have been added. A blending stump is a tight paper stick used to smudge and soften pencil, charcoal or pastel so edges look smooth instead of scratchy. That one change tells you two things. First, the check sheet is the document that actually governs your materials, so get the current copy from your advisor and read it line by line. Second, and this matters: the section of the guideline titled “Competitors Must Provide” did not survive into this extract — the list is missing from the text this page was built from. So nothing here can tell you what to pack. Do not pack from memory and do not copy a friend from a different event. Get the sheet.

    What Judges Score

    A judge scores your finished poster against a rubric. A rubric is a scoring chart: each row is one thing being judged, and the columns are how well you did it — Excellent, Good, Average, Fair, Poor. The first two rows of this rubric are worth 25 points each at Excellent, then 20, 15, 10, and 0 as the work falls off. The rubric then starts a second section where Excellent is worth 20 points, but this extract is cut off before those rows are named. That means there are scored criteria on the official rating sheet that are not shown below. Get them, and all the point values, from the guideline PDF. If two competitors tie, the guideline says the tie is broken using the rating sheet sections with the highest point value, in descending order — the biggest section first.
