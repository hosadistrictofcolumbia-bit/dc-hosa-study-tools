First Step Career Skills

Your team picks one career skill from a list of twelve, films a short role-play video that shows that skill being used in a healthcare setting, uploads it, and then talks to judges for five minutes about what you made and what you learned.

You submit
Two things go into the HOSA Digital Upload System, and ONE team member uploads both. First: the Copyright Form, uploaded as ONE combined PDF. A PDF is a file that looks the same on every device; "combined" means everything is inside a single file, not several. Second: the LINK to your role-playing video, uploaded separately as a hyperlink. A hyperlink is just a web address you click. The video itself is not uploaded as a file. The link must be set to public so the judges can open it.
Rounds
Round One is your role-playing video. It is pre-judged, which means judges watch it before the conference on their own, using the link you uploaded. It can be no longer than two and a half minutes (2.5 minutes). Round Two is a five (5) minute presentation to a panel of judges at the conference, where you explain your skill set and how you made the video. Every team that uploads by the deadline advances to Round Two. Your pre-judged video score is added to your presentation score to decide the final results.
Dress
Proper business attire or the official HOSA uniform. Bonus points will be awarded for proper dress. All team members must be in proper dress for the team to receive the bonus points. If one person on the team dresses wrong, nobody gets the points.

The DC HOSA calendar — read this before the guideline’s dates

Stage 1 · SLC — binding first

DC HOSA collects uploads and pre-judged material by the end of the first week of February. What you submit then must be final and complete — every required element, in the right format. It is judged exactly as submitted. The State Leadership Conference itself is in March, but the upload closes over a month before it. This is the deadline DC competitors miss.

Stage 2 · ILC — only if you qualify

Qualify at SLC and you may revise and improve the work. DC HOSA’s ILC upload deadline is generally around 1 May, deliberately ahead of the national date, so there is time to confirm every file landed in the right place and fix it if it did not.

For reference · the national deadline

The guideline states: "May 15 at midnight EST is the final deadline, and there will be NO EXCEPTIONS." That is the national ILC deadline, and the guideline adds that teams who do not upload are not eligible for Round Two and are not given a competition appointment time at ILC.

Work to the DC dates, not this one. DC’s deadlines shift a little year to year — confirm the exact dates with your chapter advisor or the state advisor before you plan around them.

New for 2026-2027. Editorial changes have been made.
Read this before anything else. Two things decide whether this event goes well for you, and both of them are about paperwork, not acting. First, the video is a hard two and a half minutes (2.5 minutes) maximum, and your title and your credits count inside that time. Second, the upload is two separate pieces: the Copyright Form goes in as ONE combined PDF, and the link to your video goes in separately as a hyperlink set to public. The guideline is blunt about what happens if you get that wrong: "If the upload is not submitted as a single PDF document, the judge will NOT view it and NO points will be awarded," and a link a judge cannot open earns nothing either. The Copyright Form asks for your video title, your school, the printed or typed names of every team member, the date, and a YES or NO answer about whether your video used any copyright-protected music, logos, images, characters, or symbols. Copyright means someone owns that music or logo and you need their permission to use it. If your answer is YES, you need written permission, and the guideline says the responsibility for getting it belongs to the team AND the chapter advisor together. That means an adult has to help you, and asking an adult for something in May is how teams miss deadlines. Start the form in the fall, not the spring.

    Two scores are added together. Judges watch your video before the conference and score it. Then a different panel scores your five-minute presentation at the conference. The pre-judged video score plus the presentation score is your final result.     Royalty-free music

    Only if your video uses recorded music

    Recorded music in a video is not covered by the Fair Practice Act or any educational exemption. Royalty-free music is usually stock, instrumental music you buy for a single fee with no payments afterward. The guideline suggests searching "Royalty Free Music," and also points out that your school media center or TV production class may already own music you are allowed to use, or you may be able to work with a local TV or radio station or a video production company.
