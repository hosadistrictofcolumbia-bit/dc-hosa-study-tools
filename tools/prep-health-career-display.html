Health Career Display

Two of you pick ONE health career, build a tri-fold display board about it, upload a slide outline of that board plus a photo of the finished board, and then stand with the board at the conference and give a five-minute talk about the career.

You submit
One combined PDF of the display board outline, uploaded by ONE member of the team to the HOSA Digital Upload System by the DC HOSA upload deadline. The outline is built in PowerPoint slide style (any platform — PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva) and is no more than 16 slides, not counting reference slides. One of those slides must be a photo of your completed display board. At the conference you bring the physical display, stand with it during the required Project Display Time, and — if you advance — give a five-minute presentation to judges. Photo ID is required. Notecards, paper or electronic, are optional.
Rounds
Round One is the digital outline and the photo of the completed display, pre-judged virtually before the conference. Round Two is a five-minute prepared oral presentation to judges. Only the top-scoring teams from Round One advance to Round Two; how many advance depends on Round One criteria, and on the space and time available. Separate from both rounds, EVERY team at ILC must attend the required Project Display Time and stand with its board. Round One and Round Two scores are added together for the final result.
Dress
Proper business attire or official HOSA uniform. Bonus points will be awarded for proper dress. Both team members must be properly dressed for the team to receive the bonus points.

The DC HOSA calendar — read this before the guideline’s dates

Stage 1 · SLC — binding first

DC HOSA collects uploads and pre-judged material by the end of the first week of February. What you submit then must be final and complete — every required element, in the right format. It is judged exactly as submitted. The State Leadership Conference itself is in March, but the upload closes over a month before it. This is the deadline DC competitors miss.

Stage 2 · ILC — only if you qualify

Qualify at SLC and you may revise and improve the work. DC HOSA’s ILC upload deadline is generally around 1 May, deliberately ahead of the national date, so there is time to confirm every file landed in the right place and fix it if it did not.

For reference · the national deadline

The guideline states: "May 15 at midnight EST is the final deadline, and there will be NO EXCEPTIONS." That is HOSA's national ILC upload deadline.

Work to the DC dates, not this one. DC’s deadlines shift a little year to year — confirm the exact dates with your chapter advisor or the state advisor before you plan around them.

New for 2026-2027. Pre-judging and advancement to Round Two has been clarified. Editorial changes have been made.
Read this before anything else. Your display board has to be finished before you upload, not after. The outline you submit at the February upload must include "one photo of their completed display board." So the board is not a summer-into-spring project you finish the week of the conference — it has to be built, mounted and photographed while there is still time to upload. Then measure it. Once the board and everything else is sitting on the table, the whole display may be no more than 48 inches wide, 24 inches deep and 36 inches tall, measured from the tabletop to the highest point of anything on the display. The tri-fold board itself is 36 inches tall, which means the 36-inch height limit is already used up by the board — nothing can stick up above it. Nothing may hang off the edge of the table either, because the next team's table may be very close. "Displays which do not adhere to these dimensions will be assessed penalty points in Round Two," and anything outside the limits is not judged at all. Measure the assembled display at home with a tape measure, models and props included, before you take the photo.

    What Judges Score

    Round One is pre-judged virtually in early June from your uploaded PDF alone — no one meets your team. Round Two is the five-minute presentation, and finalists are announced on-site at ILC. "Scores from Round One will be added to Round Two to determine the final results." A tie is broken by the rating sheet section with the highest point value, then the next highest, in descending order.
