Health Education

Your team picks a health topic, builds a real lesson about it, teaches that lesson to a real audience, measures whether the audience learned anything, and documents the whole thing in a portfolio.

You submit
One combined PDF of the portfolio, uploaded by ONE member of the team to the HOSA Digital Upload System. At the conference the team gives a five-minute presentation about the lesson. Photo ID is required. Index cards or electronic notecards are optional.
Rounds
Round One is the digital portfolio, pre-judged virtually before the conference. Round Two is a five-minute presentation to judges at the conference. Every team that uploads a portfolio by the upload deadline gets a Round Two appointment time. The two scores are added together for the final result.
Dress
Proper business attire or official HOSA uniform. Bonus points are awarded for proper dress. All team members must be properly dressed for the team to receive the bonus points.

The DC HOSA calendar — read this before the guideline’s dates

Stage 1 · SLC — binding first

DC HOSA collects uploads and pre-judged material by the end of the first week of February. What you submit then must be final and complete — every required element, in the right format. It is judged exactly as submitted. The State Leadership Conference itself is in March, but the upload closes over a month before it. This is the deadline DC competitors miss.

Stage 2 · ILC — only if you qualify

Qualify at SLC and you may revise and improve the work. DC HOSA’s ILC upload deadline is generally around 1 May, deliberately ahead of the national date, so there is time to confirm every file landed in the right place and fix it if it did not.

For reference · the national deadline

The guideline states: "May 15 at midnight EST is the final deadline, and there will be NO EXCEPTIONS." That is HOSA's national ILC date for material submitted through the Digital Upload System.

Work to the DC dates, not this one. DC’s deadlines shift a little year to year — confirm the exact dates with your chapter advisor or the state advisor before you plan around them.

New for 2026-2027. Editorial changes have been made.
Read this before anything else. You have to actually teach the lesson. The guideline says the team "will determine a target audience for their lesson and must present it to a real audience." This is not a lesson plan contest — it is a teaching contest, and the plan is only the setup. Everything downstream depends on the lesson really happening: the title page has to state your audience's age or grade level and the number of participants, the Materials section is where photographs of the lesson live, and the Feedback Tools section only exists because you gave your audience something to fill out. Teams lose here in a very specific way — they write a beautiful lesson plan, teach it to four friends at lunch on the last weekend, and have nothing to show for it. Book a real audience early: a health class, an elementary classroom, a club meeting, a senior center, a scout troop. Get an adult's permission in writing. Take photos while you teach. Collect the evaluations on the spot, because you cannot go back and get them later.

    Round One scores the portfolio, pre-judged before anyone meets you. Round Two scores the five-minute presentation. The two scores are added to determine final results. If there is a tie, it is broken by the rating sheet section with the highest point value, working down.
